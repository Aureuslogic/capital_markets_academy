Trading Psychology

Why the market does not beat most traders

Most retail accounts do not fail from a lack of indicators. They fail from decisions made in the wrong state. Here is the pattern, and what actually breaks it.

By Sam Onigbanjo, founder of Capital Markets Academy and creator of the EIT™ framework · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read
The short answerMost traders lose because of behaviour, not analysis: revenge trading after a loss, overtrading after a win, oversized positions and moved stop losses. These are emotional habits, so they are fixed by a behavioural system with live feedback and accountability, not by another strategy.

Ask a struggling trader what went wrong and you will usually hear about the market. It was choppy. The news hit. The level did not hold. Ask them to show you their worst week and a different story appears. The market moved against them, as markets do. Then they made five decisions in a row that turned a normal loss into a damaging one.

The pattern behind most blown accounts

The details differ, but the sequence is remarkably consistent:

  • A loss lands. It is within plan, but it stings.
  • Revenge trading begins. The next trade is taken to recover money, not because a setup exists.
  • Size creeps up. Smaller positions feel too slow to fix the problem.
  • The stop moves. Closing at a loss now feels like admitting defeat, so the trade is given "more room".
  • The day ends badly. One planned loss has become several unplanned ones.

The reverse happens too. A strong win produces overconfidence, and overtrading gives the gains back. Either way, the analysis was rarely the problem.

There are thousands of courses teaching entries and exits. Very few teach the trader how to govern their own behaviour when real money moves against them.

Why more strategy does not fix it

When results disappoint, the natural response is to look for a better system. More indicators, a new course, a different timeframe. But a new strategy run by the same person, in the same state, tends to produce the same week. The habit travels with the trader.

Revenge trading and overtrading are emotional habits. Habits are changed by noticing them early, having a rule that interrupts them, and practising that rule until it holds under pressure.

What changes the pattern

Three things make the difference in practice, and they work best together.

  • A pre-trade rule for your state. The EIT™ Traffic Light Check rates you before the chart, and decides whether you may trade at all.
  • Live feedback. Trading alongside experienced traders in real market conditions shows you your habits as they happen, not weeks later in a spreadsheet.
  • Accountability. Structured groups where you report your state and your rule-breaks. It is far harder to ignore a habit that other people are watching for.

This is the thinking behind Capital Markets Academy. We do not only ask "did you make money?" We ask "did you trade intelligently?" Over a long enough period, the second question is the one that protects the first.

Where to start

Pick your last three losing days. For each one, write down what you did after the first loss. If the same move appears more than once, you have found the habit that costs you most. That is the one to build a rule around first.

Key takeaways

  • Most losing patterns are behavioural: revenge trading, overtrading, oversizing and moved stops.
  • A better strategy run in the same state tends to produce the same result.
  • Rules for your state, live feedback and accountability change the habit at its source.
  • Start by finding the move you repeat after your first loss of the day.
