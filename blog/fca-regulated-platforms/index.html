Safety

Why we train only on FCA-regulated platforms

Education itself is not regulated. The platform holding your money is. That is why safety is where serious training starts, not where it ends.

By Sam Onigbanjo, founder of Capital Markets Academy and creator of the EIT™ framework · 31 October 2026 · 5 min read
The short answerCapital Markets Academy trains students only on UK-based, FCA-authorised platforms because the regulator sets rules on how those firms treat retail clients and handle their money. Before opening any account, check the firm on the FCA Financial Services Register and the FCA Warning List.

There is no shortage of places to trade. Some are household names, some are new, and some exist mainly to take deposits. From the outside, especially on social media, they can look almost identical. A good chart and a slick app say nothing about who is holding your money or what rules they follow.

That is why, from the first lesson, we train members exclusively on UK-based platforms authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority.

What regulation does, and does not, do

FCA authorisation means a firm must meet the regulator’s standards on how it treats customers, how it handles client money and how it describes risk. For retail clients trading CFDs and spread bets, firms must also apply protections such as limits on leverage, automatic close-out when margin runs low, and protection against losing more than the money in your account.

Regulation does not stop you losing money. Leveraged products are high risk, and most retail accounts trading them lose money. What it does is remove a separate, avoidable risk: dealing with a firm that is not held to any standard at all.

Your strategy deals with market risk. Your choice of platform deals with everything else.

How to check a firm in five minutes

  • Search the firm on the FCA Financial Services Register. Check the exact legal name, the firm reference number and that its status is authorised.
  • Check the FCA Warning List for unauthorised firms and known scams.
  • Make sure the website address and phone number you are using match those on the Register. Fraudsters copy the names of real firms.
  • Read the firm’s own risk warning. Every regulated CFD provider must publish the percentage of its retail accounts that lose money.

The FCA’s guide on how to check a firm is authorised walks through each step.

Warning signs worth taking seriously

  • Promises of guaranteed or unusually high returns.
  • Pressure to deposit quickly, or to deposit more to "unlock" a withdrawal.
  • Contact that began on social media or messaging apps, offering to trade for you.
  • A firm you cannot find on the Register, or one whose contact details do not match it.

If something feels wrong, stop and check. A legitimate firm will still be there tomorrow.

Key takeaways

  • Trading education is not regulated; trading platforms are.
  • FCA authorisation brings rules on client treatment, client money and risk disclosure.
  • Regulation reduces avoidable risk. It does not remove market risk.
  • Check every firm on the FCA Register and Warning List before you deposit.
